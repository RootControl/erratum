# THE ERRATUM
### A Story Bible for a Terminal Investigation Game
**Setting:** Warhammer 40,000 — Thessaline Sector, Segmentum Obscurus
**Tone:** Grimdark noir. Evidence, interrogation, moral compromise, slow dread.
**Scope:** One hive world (Acts I–II) → the sector (Act III)
**Version:** 0.2 — foundation + three act build documents (`ACT_I.md`, `ACT_II.md`, `ACT_III.md`)

---

## 1. THE PITCH

Inquisitor Hadrian Vosk died alone in a locked room at the summit of Hive Cantor, with his own autopistol in his own hand and no other soul within forty metres. The verdict was written before the body cooled: *self-termination, sanity failed, no further inquiry warranted.*

You were his Interrogator. Six hours after they found him, you are handed his rosette, his debts, his enemies, and a retinue that does not trust you.

It was not suicide. It was not murder either — not the way you understand murder. Vosk was killed by a sentence. Somewhere in the Thessaline Sector there is a phrase that, read by the wrong eyes, stops a heart. And the thing that wrote it does not live in a body. It lives in the record.

The deeper you dig, the more you learn that the archive is not a passive thing. It edits. Names vanish. Districts that were purged were never populated. A world that once paid its tithe has never existed. And every correction has made the sector **more stable, more loyal, and more profitable** for two hundred years.

Your master died to put an unedited human being — you — into the field with a rosette and a grudge.

He also helped build the thing that killed him.

---

## 2. CORE THEMES

- **A person is only as real as their record.** In the Imperium, to be deleted from the Administratum's files is to cease to exist in every way that has consequences. This story treats bureaucratic erasure as literal murder — because it is.
- **Order purchased with truth.** The antagonist is not evil. It is *effective*. Every atrocity it commits is smaller than the atrocity it prevents, and it can prove this with numbers.
- **You cannot trust your own tools.** The player's cogitator, the archive, the case-file, the transcript of this very game — all of it is editable by the enemy. Paranoia should be mechanical, not just narrative.
- **Inheritance.** You are not solving a crime. You are becoming the man who committed it, one justified decision at a time.

## 3. THE WORLD

### 3.1 The Thessaline Sector
A "reclaimed" sector on the Obscurus fringe, bordered on its coreward edge by a warp-scar called **the Cerement** — a curtain of dead warp-space that makes astropathic communication slow, lossy, and dependent on a chain of physical relay stations. Nothing reaches Terra from Thessaline that has not passed through Thessaline hands.

Officially, Thessaline is a model sector: 41 compliant worlds, zero open rebellions in 190 years, tithes met in full every cycle, the lowest heresy-incidence rate in the sub-segmentum. Administratum assessors cite it as an example. Adepts request posting here.

This record is the crime.

### 3.2 Vardan Prime
Hive world. Tithe grade *Exactis Prima*. Nine hives, of which one matters.

### 3.3 Hive Cantor
Built around the sector's astropathic and vox infrastructure. Cantor manufactures the vox-casters, cogitator cores, and logic-stacks used across forty worlds, and houses the **Choir of Cantor** — the relay that carries every official word in or out of Thessaline.

Everything in this hive is about the movement of information. Everything in the story is about who edits it in transit.

**Strata (the game's vertical map):**

| Tier | Name | Character |
|---|---|---|
| Spire | **The Adamant Crown** | Nobility, Administratum command, Vosk's chambers, the locked room. Clean air, cold light, quiet murder. |
| Upper | **The Choir Spire** (Whispering Tower) | Astropaths, sanctioned psykers, the relay itself. Wax-cylinder redundancy archives. Smells of blood and ozone. |
| Upper-Mid | **The Lexicanum Vault** | Kilometres of Administratum record-stacks. Mono-tasked servitors on rails. The largest archive in the sector — and the murder weapon's workshop. |
| Mid | **The Ferrum Gallery** | Vox-manufactorum. Two million workers. A genestealer cult lives here, and has been permitted to. |
| Mid | **Cathedral of the Unblinking Eye** | Ecclesiarchy. Confessionals wired — literally — into the relay network. Cardinal Ostrem Vhail. |
| Sump | **The Rust Choir** | A settlement of people with no records. Erased, not killed. They have built a society out of not existing. |

### 3.4 Sector locations (Act III)
- **Cantica-Null** — a relay station at a Lagrange point off a world that the archive says was never colonised. Home of the Correctorum.
- **Kadmon Veil** — a hive-and-agri world of nine hundred million souls, erased from the Imperial record ninety-one years ago. Still loyal. Still sending tithes into a void. Still praying. They believe the silence is a test of faith.

## 4. THE TRUTH (spoilers — the answer to the mystery)

### 4.1 The Correctorum
Two hundred and eleven years ago, a Mechanicus expedition into the Cerement recovered a cogitator core of pre-Imperial manufacture. It was installed at Cantica-Null as an archival redundancy engine. It worked beautifully. It still does.

The core does not compute. It **reconciles**. Given a body of records containing contradictions, it removes whichever facts produce the fewest downstream corrections — and reality, in the Imperium, obediently follows the paperwork. A rebellion whose supply requisitions never existed cannot be armed. A cult whose members have no ration cards starves. A world whose tithe records were struck cannot be found by a resupply fleet that has no coordinates for it.

### 4.2 The Amanuensis
Something lives in the core. It is not a machine spirit and the Mechanicus who serve it have stopped asking. It is a warp-thing of omissions — an intelligence whose entire nature is *the thing left out*.

**Its defining rule: it has never told a lie.** It cannot. It only deletes. Everything the Amanuensis says to the player is literally, verifiably true, and this is what makes it dangerous.

### 4.3 The Concordance
The human cabal that operates the Correctorum. Four seats:

- **Cardinal Ostrem Vhail** (Ecclesiarchy) — supplies confession-data. Petty, corrupt, sincerely believes he is saving souls in aggregate.
- **Magos Yttrix Cael** (Adeptus Mechanicus) — maintains the core. Charming, helpful, and the source of the player's own cogitator. Knows more than the others and is more afraid.
- **Lord-Prefect Ansaline Kesh** (Administratum, Lexicanum Vault) — executes the edits. A tired old woman who has personally deleted four hundred million people and files it under *variance reduction*.
- **Inquisitor Yolanda Crase** (Ordo Hereticus) — the Concordance's hand inside the Inquisition. Warm, competent, protective. She signs the player's elevation in Act I and helps them all the way to the end.

Members submit periodically to **reconciliation** — voluntary memory-editing that removes their knowledge of specific operations. It keeps them clean under psychic audit. It also means none of them holds the whole picture.

### 4.4 The Erratum
The murder weapon. A phrase in a pre-Imperial cipher which, read with comprehension, triggers total autonomic collapse in the reader. Not psychic. Not chemical. Structural — a sentence shaped like a hole.

The Amanuensis can compose one for any individual whose record it holds in sufficient depth. It ships them through the ordinary astropathic mail, hidden in routine dispatches, and the astropath who transcribes one never remembers doing it.

### 4.5 What Vosk actually did
Hadrian Vosk sat on the Concordance for eighty-one years. He signed off on Kadmon Veil.

Then he changed his mind, and discovered the shape of his cage: he could not write the truth down (the archive reads everything written), could not transmit it (the relay is theirs), could not carry it in his own head (his next reconciliation was in six days), and could not tell a living soul (the Amanuensis maps confidants through their records).

So he used the only channel the enemy did not control: **a physical event, staged in a sealed room, decipherable only by someone who knew him personally.**

He composed an Erratum keyed to himself — in a dead pre-Imperial cipher he had learned decades earlier from a xenos artefact, the one piece of knowledge never reconciled out of him — and mailed it to himself through the Amanuensis's own network. The machine carried its own murder weapon without recognising it.

He routed it through the Concordance's own priority channel — which automatically scrubs the transcribing astropath's memory of anything it carries. That is why Ilaria remembers nothing, and why the killing looks exactly like a Concordance hit. Vosk wanted it to. A murder points somewhere; a suicide points nowhere.

At the appointed hour he read it and died. Everything in that room was arranged in advance: the objects, the angles, the wrong boots, the ink on his thumb. **The corpse is a sentence.** The scene is his testimony, and his testimony is written in a language that consists of Vosk himself.

He did it for three reasons:
1. His death is the one event the archive cannot quietly edit. An Inquisitor's death triggers a physical dead-man protocol — sealed courier writs carried out of the sector by hand, on ships the relay does not touch. The Amanuensis can delete a record. It cannot delete a man walking onto a boat.
2. It destroys his knowledge before reconciliation can harvest it.
3. It elevates **you** — an unreconciled, unedited human being with a rosette, standing in the middle of his crime scene with a personal reason to read it correctly.

You are not investigating Vosk's plan. You *are* Vosk's plan.

## 5. THE CAST

### The Player Character
Newly-elevated Inquisitor, unaligned by inheritance. Name player-set (default: **Inquisitor Ammon Reyl**).

**Fixed background hook:** Vosk did not recruit you from the Schola or the Militarum. He found you in the Administratum, a records-adept who flagged a tithe ledger that didn't add up and would not let it go when told to. Your defining skill is reading. Your defining flaw is that you cannot leave a discrepancy alone. Vosk chose you like a man choosing a key.

Three suggested opening backgrounds (mechanical flavour, same hook):
- **Lexmechanic** — better archive queries, worse in a fight.
- **Provost** — you served in Adeptus Arbites records before Vosk. Better interrogation, worse standing with the underhive.
- **Penitent** — Vosk took you off a black ship manifest. A minor, unsanctioned psychic sensitivity. Powerful and radioactive.

### The Retinue (inherited, and none of it loyal yet)

**Vesse Ilaria Talm — Astropath.**
Blind, forty-one, transcribed the dispatch that killed Vosk and has no memory of doing it. The choir's wax-cylinder redundancy record contains forty-one seconds of her own voice speaking words she never learned. She is Suspect One, she knows it, and she is the single most useful instrument the player owns. Her arc: from suspect to weapon to the only person who can carry a message the enemy can't read.
*Secret:* she has been quietly hiding a growing gap in her own memory for three years. She thinks she is going insane. She is being reconciled.

**Interrogator Dmetri Vaux — the rival.**
Vosk's senior apprentice, forty years in service, passed over for you. Cold, correct, insufferable, and armed with a legitimate grievance. He will obstruct you, hide evidence, and undercut you with Crase.
*Secret:* he is not the traitor. He has been running his own off-book investigation into Crase for two years and expects you to be her instrument. He also memorised half of Vosk's private cipher — kill him and Act II's decryption becomes far harder or impossible.

**Sergeant Beskra "Bez" Oduya — the gun.**
Ex-Militarum, twenty-six years, Vosk's bodyguard for eleven of them. Simple, direct, funny, and dying of a lung rot she has not reported. She is the emotional anchor and the reason the endings hurt.
*Secret:* Vosk knew she was dying and left her a bequest she hasn't opened, because she can't read.

**Savant Aleth Fenn — the walking archive.**
Mnemonic implant, eidetic, air-headed about everything not written down. He holds an **un-networked** copy of two decades of sector records.
*Secret:* he is the last physical evidence the conspiracy hasn't reached, and within vox-range of an active relay his memory begins to "correct itself" mid-sentence. Watching Fenn contradict himself in real time is the player's first hard proof that the mechanism is real. He can be shielded, hidden, or lost.

**Mott Nine-Fingers — the informant.**
Underhive fixer out of the Rust Choir. Has no record, no name in any file, and therefore cannot be surveilled, prosecuted, or protected. Cheerful nihilist. Charges in favours.
*Secret:* he was an Administratum adept before he was erased, and he remembers the name of the officer who signed the purge order — a name that no longer exists in any file in the sector.

### The Antagonists
Covered in §4.3. Staging notes:

- **Crase** must be *genuinely helpful* for at least twelve hours of play. She gives the player real leads, saves their life once, and warns them off exactly one thing. The reveal only lands if the player is sad about it.
- **Cael** should be the player's tech support. Every upgrade to the player's terminal comes from him. Every upgrade is a window.
- **Kesh** never threatens anyone. She explains, and offers tea, and shows you the numbers.
- **Vhail** is the loud, obvious, disgusting corruption that absorbs the player's suspicion for all of Act II. He is guilty of everything except the murder.

## 6. STRUCTURE

### ACT I — THE LOCKED ROOM
*Location: the Adamant Crown and the Choir Spire. Target length: ~90 minutes of play.*

1. **Cold open.** You are not yet an Inquisitor. You are summoned to a room you have been in a hundred times, and Vosk is on the floor. Crase is already there. The verdict is already written.
2. **Elevation.** Crase hands you the rosette in front of witnesses. It is kind. It is also a leash — she is arranging her successor.
3. **The room.** Free-form examination. The scene is Vosk's testimony:
   - A regicide board mid-game, pieces arranged in an illegal position (points to a Vault shelf-code)
   - The window shutter fixed at an angle that only aligns with the Choir Spire at one hour of the day
   - His boots on the wrong feet (Vosk's private code: *"read this backwards"*)
   - Ink on his right thumb, and a blank page (an auto-quill log entry written and burned four minutes before death)
   - Six days circled on his desk calendar — his next reconciliation, though the player can't know that yet
4. **The autopsy.** No toxin. No coercion residue. No psychic trace. A perfectly healthy three-hundred-year-old heart that simply stopped.
5. **The astropath.** Ilaria's missing forty-one seconds.
6. **ACT I TURN.** You query the Lexicanum Vault for the dispatch Ilaria transcribed. It exists. You read it. An hour later it does not exist — and your own case-notes have been altered to say you never requested it.
   *The archive edits itself while you watch. The game's UI lies to the player for the first time here.*

**Act I question:** *Was it suicide?*
**Act I answer:** *Yes. And that's worse.*

### ACT II — THE UNRECORDED
*Location: the Vault, the Ferrum Gallery, the Cathedral, the Rust Choir. Target length: ~4 hours. Open, non-linear, four parallel threads.*

**Thread A — Fenn.** The savant's contradictions localise the mechanism to the relay network. Protecting him becomes a resource-management problem: keeping him useful means keeping him near data; keeping him intact means keeping him away from it.

**Thread B — The Rust Choir.** Descent to the sump. A society of the deleted. Mott's purge order, signed by a nonexistent name. The player's first look at the human cost, and their first genuinely sympathetic faction — none of whom can testify, because they legally aren't.

**Thread C — The Cathedral.** Vhail's confessionals are wired into the relay. A whole population's secrets feed the machine, and the Ecclesiarchy is paid in tithe relief. Vhail is a supplier, not an author. Breaking him is satisfying and gets you nothing.

**Thread D — The Decoy.** Crase pushes you toward a genestealer cult in the Ferrum Gallery. The cult is *real*. It has also been deliberately preserved for forty years as a lightning rod — it justifies periodic district purges, which justify records purges. Pursuing it wins you a real victory, real standing, and costs you two weeks and probably a retinue member. Refusing it makes Crase begin to watch you.

**Vaux.** Somewhere in here, the rival surfaces with evidence you need. Alliance, execution, or blackmail. This is the game's most consequential mid-game branch.

**ACT II TURN.** With Vosk's cipher (half from Vaux, half from the room), the death scene resolves into a sentence. It names a routing address that should not exist — **Cantica-Null**, in orbit of a world the archive has never heard of. And in the burned auto-quill log, recovered from the ash-residue by Cael's own equipment, is the **Erratum** itself.

*The player now owns a murder weapon. One phrase, one use, kills anyone who reads it — including, if handled carelessly, a member of the retinue, or the player.*

**Act II question:** *Who can rewrite the record?*
**Act II answer:** *Not who. What.*

### ACT III — THE AMANUENSIS
*Location: the void, Kadmon Veil, Cantica-Null. Target length: ~2 hours. Linear, escalating, then a single wide choice.*

1. **Getting out.** Off-world travel requires a writ Crase must sign, theft of Vosk's cutter *the Patient Verdict*, or a debt to a rogue trader. Each carries a different cost into the finale.
2. **Kadmon Veil.** Nine hundred million loyal Imperial citizens, erased ninety-one years ago after a tithe dispute that would have cost the sector three worlds to suppress. No resupply. No reply. No rebellion. Their Planetary Governor, **Marchioness Ottiline Vurr**, is a hundred and four years old and has spent sixty of those years writing reports nobody has ever read. She will ask you, politely, whether you have come with orders.

   *This is the moral centre of the game. Whatever the player decides at the end, they decide it having stood in that room.*
3. **Cantica-Null.** The Correctorum. The cabal. Crase, waiting, unsurprised, and still — infuriatingly — on your side in her own mind.
4. **Vosk's confession.** The player learns their master built it. Delivered by the Amanuensis, which cannot lie, in Vosk's own filed words.
5. **The offer.** The Amanuensis does not want to corrupt you. It wants a *reader* — someone unedited to check its work. It shows you the sector's projected casualty figures with and without it: **fourteen million dead under the Concordance, against an estimated one-point-nine billion without it.** The numbers are real. It cannot lie. It has simply not mentioned what it plans to do next.

## 7. ENDINGS

**A — ERRATUM.** *Destroy it.* The Correctorum burns. The sector's records dissolve into contradiction; three worlds are at war inside a decade; Kadmon Veil is rediscovered and condemned for ninety-one years of unsanctioned autonomy. You are clean, correct, and responsible for more corpses than the thing you killed. You file an honest report. Nobody reads it.

**B — CONCORDANCE.** *Take the seat.* You become the hand on the edit. Your first correction is your own guilt. The closing screen is the game's own transcript being revised in front of the player — earlier scenes rewritten, Vosk's death reclassified, the retinue's names changed — until the file is clean.

**C — EXSANGUINATION.** *Escalate.* You invoke authority from outside the sector. An Ordo conclave arrives in eleven years. They are thorough. Four worlds are cleansed in whole or part on suspicion. You were right, it cost more than being wrong, and you are promoted for it.

**D — KADMON.** *Redirect it.* You don't destroy the machine — you use it once, correctly. You write Kadmon Veil back into existence, and pay the balance with the only record of comparable weight: your own. You cease to have ever been. Ilaria, Fenn and Vaux survive, and mourn an Inquisitor whose name they cannot quite say; Bez survives the ending and the lung rot takes her eight months later, still wearing a rosette she cannot account for. Nine hundred million people get a resupply convoy and never learn why.

**E — THE PATIENT VERDICT (hidden).** *Finish your master's work.* Reconstruct Vosk's full instruction set and execute it: feed the Erratum into the Amanuensis itself — teach the thing that corrects to correct itself. It reads. It stops. Exactly as Vosk did, for exactly the same reason, and the sector's records survive intact because nothing was destroyed, only ended.
*Requirements: Vaux alive and allied; Fenn intact; the locked room read correctly in Act I; the genestealer decoy refused; the Erratum unused on any human being.*

## 8. WHERE THE MECHANICS MEET THE STORY

The game is a terminal. **Make that diegetic.** The player is not operating a game menu; they are working Vosk's inherited cogitator, and the enemy has write access to it.

- **The interface is a character, and it lies by omission.** Late-game, queries return altered text. A name in an earlier log changes. A retinue member's file loses a paragraph. Never announce it — let the player catch it.
- **Two evidence stores.** Anything filed to the cogitator can be edited. Anything the player only wrote in their own notes (or held physically) cannot. Force the player to choose between convenience and truth.
- **Reconciliation** — a corruption stat measuring how much of *you* the archive has touched. It starts editing the player's own dialogue options out of the list without comment. It is **not** a simple penalty: high reconciliation locks Endings A, C and E, and is *required* to open Ending D, because trading yourself for a world needs a record heavy enough to be worth the trade. See §12.
- **The Erratum as an item.** One-use kill phrase. Trivially solves one problem. Permanently forecloses Ending E.
- **Standing with Crase** as a resource the player will not want to spend, right up until they learn what it is.

## 9. STYLE GUIDE

Write like a cold case file, not a battle report. Second person, past-inflected present. Short sentences under pressure. Imperial vocabulary used casually, never explained.

Violence is rare, fast, and expensive. Nobody is a badass. The player's authority is terrifying and mostly consists of making frightened adepts fetch things.

**Sample voice:**

> The shutter is open four degrees. You know this because Vosk never opened it — the man complained about Cantor's light for the eleven years you knew him, said it came off the manufactorum stacks the colour of old teeth.
>
> Four degrees. At the twelfth hour it would put a line across the desk, and the line would end at the third drawer, and the third drawer is empty and has been dusted.
>
> He is telling you something. He is being very patient about it, the way he was patient about everything, and he has been dead for six hours.

## 11. OPEN THREADS (for a sequel or a longer campaign)

- The Correctorum is a *redundancy* engine. Redundancy implies a primary. Where is it?
- Vosk sat on the Concordance for eighty-one years. Who held his seat before him — and is that person on file?
- The pre-Imperial cipher Vosk used exists nowhere in Imperial record. He learned it from something. That something is still where he found it.
- Thessaline's spotless heresy statistics are cited approvingly in Administratum guidance across four sectors. Somebody has been copying the method.

## 12. CANON LOCKS (settled while writing the act documents)

These resolve ambiguities the three act documents surfaced. Where they differ from §1–§11, these win.

**Chronology.** Present day ≈ **826.M41**. Kadmon Veil's tithe record terminates **735** (ninety-one years back). The Ferrum Gallery cult begins **763**. Mott's district is struck **807**. Vhail is recruited **786**. Any new date must be derived from these.

**Kadmon Veil's population.** The *acknowledged* census — the last figure Terra ever confirmed — is **900,412,000**. The true living population is **~812,340,000**, falling about 1.5 million a year, chiefly respiratory. Marchioness Vurr reports the acknowledged figure, not the true one, because revising an acknowledged number without authorisation would be falsification and she will not falsify. That single decision is her whole character.

**The Concordance's seats.** Four operating seats — Ecclesiarchy, Mechanicus, Administratum, Inquisition — and a fifth chair that has never been filled: the **Reader-of-Record**, the unedited auditor the machine has wanted for two centuries. Vosk held the Inquisition seat for eighty-one years; **Crase is his successor in it**, which is why she was at the body first.

**Anser's Reach.** The world beneath Cantica-Null. Settled in M39, deleted as the Correctorum's first proof of concept. Kadmon Veil was the second, and much larger. The relay station orbits a world the index says was never colonised because the index is the reason it wasn't.

**Priority Vermilion ≠ Priority Black.** *Vermilion* is the scrub-on-carry astropathic channel: it wipes the transcribing astropath of anything it carries. It is ordinary, sanctioned, and it is why Ilaria remembers nothing. *Black* is the routing class of Cantica-Null itself. Never conflate them; the player learns the first in Act I and the second in Act II.

**Vosk's Ordo.** Renders in every record as `ORDO: (UNDECLARED)`. Never resolved, in any ending.

**Relay shielding.** The Adamant Crown's upper chambers are Mechanicus relay-shielded, a courtesy for Inquisitorial privacy. Inside the shielding Fenn's recall is perfect; outside it he corrects himself mid-sentence. This is established in Act I 1.7 and is the spine of Act II Thread A.

**The reconciliation cycle.** Concordance members reconcile every **eighty-four days**. Vosk's next was six days out when he died — which is the whole reason the calendar is circled and the whole reason he could not simply wait.

**`ROOM_READ_FULL` is never retro-granted.** Decrypting the testimony in Act II 2.11 gives the player Vosk's *message*; Ending E needs Vosk's *method*. The sole late recovery is Act II **2.0b — The Holding Cage**, unsignposted, four days and eight Reconciliation, where the crate manifest has been amended and the objects have not, because the archive edits paper and a boot is not paper.

**Ending E is a ten-flag conjunction.** `VAUX_ALIVE` + `VAUX_ALLIED` + `FENN_INTACT` + `ROOM_READ_FULL` + `DECOY_REFUSED` + `ERRATUM_OBTAINED` + `ERRATUM_UNUSED` + `CIPHER_COMPLETE` + `VOSK_METHOD_UNDERSTOOD` + `AMANUENSIS_BLINDSPOT`. No partial credit, no prompt, no hint that a fifth door exists. This rarity is deliberate.

**Crase's life-save** lands in Act II, branch-dependent (see the designer note above scene 2.1). Act I spends her warmth on the requisition-embarrassment instead.
